**Airlock product research: failure cases in shared engineering workspaces**

Prepared 8 November 2026. This is a product and failure analysis, not an implementation or a field-test result. Initial research reviewed integration HEAD `c026bab` and the uncommitted integration changes present then. The publication was reconciled with main snapshot `1bbbfbf` on the same date; this is a bounded source review, not a deployment audit. Other machines and later commits may differ. Source assertions require rechecking when implementation changes. No product code, running service, hardware setting, camera, microphone, screen lock or account permission was changed.

**The product, grounded in the repository.** LOCKI AIRLOCK is an explicitly armed workstation security assistant. It combines observations around a desk with Mac USB changes and visible application launches; applies server-owned incident rules; warns locally; can request an owner-consented ordinary screen lock; and gives the owner or an authorized reviewer a shared incident record. Nemotron investigates recorded evidence through bounded tools. A separate managed-agent workflow can require exact human authorization before selected callbacks execute. The current development setup spans a Mac, an optional independent Jetson/camera, a model host and a shared backend. These are development arrangements, not a validated per-desk deployment requirement. [Repository overview](../../../README.md), [server contract](../../../spacetime/CONTRACT.md), [control-room implementation](../../../frontend/src/airlock/AirlockApp.tsx).

The operative server policy is narrower than a general intrusion detector: presence alone is a soft observation; an unapproved USB or application launch opens a review incident; a USB and person observation within ten seconds raise incident severity. The lock path is restricted to eligible USB incidents or authenticated owner panic, with additional consent, freshness and execution gates. It does not automatically lock merely because a person is unfamiliar. The documented response is screen lock, not shutdown; real screen locks are disabled by default. Local camera/voice modes are a separate path and can still produce warnings without a server incident. [Policy implementation](../../../spacetime/src/index.ts), [camera and warning behavior](../../../docs/airlock/VISION.md), [default safety limits](../../../README.md).

**Assessment.** There is a plausible useful product here: a shared, factual record of interference with an engineer's desk, an appropriate immediate response, and authorized review while the owner is away. Its value has not been demonstrated merely by connecting these components. It must outperform normal OS protection on a specific task while creating sufficiently little interruption, surveillance burden and maintenance work. There is no evidence in this review of customer demand, willingness to pay, measured loss reduction or production-grade accuracy.

The strongest hypothesis is that owners and trusted reviewers can understand and respond to meaningful changes faster, especially where independent desk observation remains available after the Mac becomes inaccessible. The managed-agent approval gate provides a different, concrete preventive boundary if actual workflows pass through it. Treat these as two independently testable benefits. They should not be blended into a promise that a camera identifies hackers or that all AI/OS actions are protected.

**The most consequential failure cases.** “Source-supported gap” means the inspected design has the stated boundary. “Failure hypothesis” means the scenario follows plausibly from that boundary but was not reproduced in a live trial. No row claims a successful exploit or a measured field failure.

|---|---|---|---|
| Critical: owner console left accessible (AUTH-06) | The UI can call the owner disarm reducer with its existing browser identity. Authenticating that identity does not establish who now holds the mouse. | A nearby person may disable protection from an already authenticated, unlocked owner session. Source-supported concern; not exploited here. | Lock before leaving; decide which controls require a fresh check or a separate trusted device. Test the exact local and remote control surfaces. |
| Critical: existing editor, terminal or browser (TAMP-01–03) | No new USB or app launch is needed. Local hand-zone warnings do not establish whole-OS action prevention. | File changes, dummy-secret access or cloud actions may occur without the main server triggers. Static coverage gap. | Make existing-session access protection the baseline. Evaluate specific monitored actions separately, without a general prevention claim. |

Measure benign correlation and intervention burden. |
| High: friend or owner causes local warning (SOC-02, HUMAN-01) | Face identification is off by default, and local presence warning behavior can occur without an owner match. | Social friction can occur even when the server correctly avoids locking. | Quiet observation for proximity; assess every audible line and its trigger. Do not solve this by assuming all friends are trusted. |
| High: authorized coworker use (AUTH-01–04) | Human friendship and permission for an exact operation are different. | Critical: post-connection warning (TAMP-05–06) | Enumeration and response occur after a device connection. Fast unwanted activity can precede the response. | A later lock cannot undo an already completed action or a copied secret. | Use native accessory/device controls as the preventive boundary; evaluate Airlock as detection and response. |
| High: baseline poisoning or benign re-enumeration (DEVICE-03–05, WORK-07) | A fresh captured identifier proves what was observed, not that the device is safe. Legitimate boards can also change identifiers. | Bad devices may appear approved; ordinary engineering work can appear unapproved. | Separate inventory, approval and hardware assurance; validate actual peripherals and normal transitions. |
| High: app identity mistaken for action safety (DEVICE-06–07, TAMP-07) | An updater can launch a new app; an approved app can do an unauthorized task; some launches are outside the observer. | False review incidents and missed harmful activity coexist. | Describe exact observation scope and avoid app-level moral labels. |
| Critical if marketed as theft prevention: laptop removal (THEFT-01–08) | Person/zone detection is not validated object tracking. Closing the lid, removing power or losing networking can remove observation and response. | Airlock can lose visibility as the laptop leaves; screen lock does not physically retain it. | Explicitly separate asset-removal detection, access protection and recovery. Prove any independent-sensor claim. |
| High: camera geometry interpreted as contact (SOC-03, VISION-01–08) | Hands near a two-dimensional zone, occlusion, multiple people and moved cameras create ambiguity. | Critical: network, watcher or power failure (FAIL-01–08) | A live camera or dashboard does not imply a live host monitor; queued events do not guarantee a deliverable lock command. | A healthy-looking system can lack the very capability the owner expects. | Define component-specific degraded states and local fallback. Verify rather than infer offline protection. |
| High: response damages work (WORK-01–03) | Security reactions can interfere with GUI automation, remote sessions, hardware flashing or screen-dependent jobs. | The product creates the loss it was meant to reduce. | No shutdown, destructive cleanup or arbitrary process termination. Validate each claimed compatible workload under ordinary screen lock. |
| High: owner return and delayed actions (STATE-03–04, COMB-04) | Events, policy changes, locks and recovery can race. | Lock loops or stale interventions make the workstation unusable. | Test final execution-time validation and a safe authenticated recovery route. |
| High: apparent protection or notification (STATE-02, REC-01, HUMAN-08) | Armed, requested, executed, confirmed, provider-accepted and human-acknowledged are different states. | Owner leaves believing an unlocked screen is locked, or assumes an unseen alert was received. | Clear states and independent outcome evidence; no success claim from speech or a queued action. |
| Critical if marketed broadly: agent gate bypass (TAMP-08) | The pre-execution gate covers only integrated tool paths. | A different agent, direct shell, browser or already-running process operates outside it. | Inventory the enforced paths and demonstrate denied callbacks there; disclose the rest. |
| High: shared-office monitoring creates a new risk (PRIV-01–08) | Cameras can see adjacent work, review roles can be too broad, and metadata can contain secrets or instructions. | Privacy loss, accidental disclosure, misuse and resistance to adoption. | Minimize capture, enforce scoped access, provide understandable retention and camera-free alternatives. |
| High: user disables an annoying system (REC-08) | Everyday legitimate actions dominate workplace activity, while repeated interruptions impose immediate costs. | The product loses practical protection even if synthetic scenarios look good. | Measure nuisance per protected hour, recovery effort and sustained voluntary use in ordinary work. |

The Mac's separate hand-zone pipeline does not remove the need for physical accuracy trials.

**What outside research changes about the product decisions.**

Apple already provides accessory authorization on supported Apple-silicon Mac laptops. New accessories can require approval, a locked Mac must be unlocked to approve them, and charging may still work when data access is refused. Airlock should complement that boundary and should not describe every connection as successful data access. [Apple accessory authorization](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102282).

Enterprise tools already offer preventive device policies: Microsoft Defender for Endpoint on macOS supports allow/deny and auditing for defined removable-media operations. Airlock's additional hypothesis is useful desk context and simpler owner/reviewer coordination, not equivalence to endpoint data-control enforcement. This comparison establishes scope; it is not a tested purchasing recommendation. [Microsoft device control](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-endpoint/mac-device-control-overview).

Apple documents that its visible-app launch notification excludes certain background applications. That reinforces the need to test existing terminals, browser sessions and background activity, rather than count an observed launch as comprehensive coverage. [Apple app-launch notification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/AppKit/NSWorkspace/didLaunchApplicationNotification).

FileVault provides data-at-rest protection; Find My is a separate device-location and recovery facility with its own setup and connectivity requirements. Neither is supplied by an Airlock screen-lock request. A claim to protect a laptop taken from the desk needs these boundaries clearly separated. [Apple FileVault](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/volume-encryption-with-filevault-sec4c6dc1b6e/1/web/1), [Apple lost-Mac guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102481).

Recognition cannot eliminate the permission problem. NIST documents image-quality and demographic differences in face matching, and its digital-authentication guidance places constraints on biometric authentication. The latter is a useful engineering reference, not a claim that Airlock is legally subject to those requirements. Since the product's default is already non-biometric, a friend-recognition database would add a separate problem without establishing permission for the next action. [NIST face-recognition evaluation](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt_demographics.html), [NIST authentication guidance](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html).

The base-rate problem matters when benign events vastly outnumber harmful ones. A detector can look accurate on a balanced demonstration while generating mostly unhelpful real alerts. Security-fatigue research also links the burden of security decisions to avoidance. These studies do not measure Airlock; they justify measuring ordinary activity and user behavior directly. [Axelsson's intrusion-detection analysis](https://people.scs.carleton.ca/~soma/id/readings/axelsson-base-rate.pdf), [NIST Security Fatigue study](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2016/09/security-fatigue/final).

For agent actions, keep authorization outside the language model. OWASP recommends checking tool permissions and parameters and treating external content as untrusted; a persuasive explanation or filtering model is not equivalent to an execution boundary. [OWASP prompt-injection prevention](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html).

**Proposed product behavior, not implemented changes.** Separate three things: observed activity, permission for that activity, and a proportionate response. A single danger score that rises whenever a stranger is nearby hides those distinctions.

| Situation | Proposed default |
|---|---|
| Owner deliberately leaves an unattended desk | Secure interactive access with the normal OS lock first; retain compatible authorized jobs. Clearly label any exceptional unlocked workflow and its residual risk. |
| Someone walks by, talks nearby, or reaches for an adjacent item | No security intervention from proximity alone. Optional minimal observation under the chosen privacy settings. |
| Owner authorizes a teammate's bounded task | Apply only that permission, for its stated duration; use appropriate OS access rather than share the owner's credentials. |
| Someone attempts computer use without permission | Maintain the existing OS access boundary; a neutral reminder or private review is appropriate when evidence supports it. No shutdown or character judgement. |
| An unapproved device or app is actually observed | Apply the explicit device/app policy and report the fact. Identity, consent for a task, device safety and malware status remain separate. |
| Equipment may have moved | Report exactly the observable fact and uncertainty; require a separately validated removal capability before claiming theft detection. |
| Sensor, model, network or notification fails | Report the specific missing coverage; apply only the verified fallback and preserve normal OS recovery. |
| A managed agent requests a sensitive operation | Require exact current authorization before its integrated callback executes. |
| Owner returns | Fast authenticated recovery; pending stale actions cannot trap the owner in a lock loop. |

For a friend who only approaches, the correct response is ordinary quiet observation. For a friend with permission to do a task, the question is whether that exact task has a valid grant. For a friend without permission to use the machine, preserving the normal lock is reasonable without deciding that the friend is a hacker. An unapproved USB may still justify a consented protective response regardless of who connected it; that is a policy decision about an action, not a moral judgement about a person.

**How to test without confusing evidence.** Score sensor truth, permission, chosen action and user harm separately. A real unknown USB is not a false sensor positive merely because it was harmless. A high-severity correlation can be a correctly executed rule that nevertheless produces an undesirable product outcome. A missing event during an outage is a coverage failure and must not disappear from the denominator.

Start with the highest-consequence cases: AUTH-06, TAMP-01–05, COMB-01, THEFT-02–03, WORK-03, FAIL-03, STATE-02–04 and REC-01. Use consenting participants, disposable data and harmless peripherals. This document does not authorize running those trials on a production workstation.

Compare normal OS protection alone against the same configuration with Airlock added. Include scheduled jobs, authorized work, unattended locked sessions and explicit unlocked exceptions. Label ground truth before examining the model output. Use separate tuning and held-out people, desks and sessions; freeze the configuration before scoring. NIST's AI risk framework emphasizes realistic, representative evaluation and context-specific tradeoffs. [NIST AI evaluation guidance](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/3-sec-characteristics/).

Measure unauthorized actions that actually completed, false user-visible interventions per protected hour and benign episode, incorrect attribution, outage duration, end-to-end response timing, lock confirmation, alert acknowledgement, recovery time and workload integrity. Report raw counts, denominators, failures, p50/p95/max timing and uncertainty. A build completing more slowly or a user turning off monitoring is a product outcome, not irrelevant noise.

Proposed hard gates are zero unauthorized protected callbacks in the specified test suite; zero automatic sensitive action solely from presence; zero shutdowns, arbitrary process kills or invented identity/intent claims; and no false report of confirmed lock or human receipt. These finite-suite gates do not prove a zero field failure rate. Statistical thresholds for misses and nuisance need an agreed exposure budget and a justified sample. For illustration, with independent equal-probability trials, zero errors in 20 trials still permits a one-sided 95% error upper bound of about 13.9%; 299 zero-error trials bring that bound just below 1%. Repeated frames and repeated trials on one person are not independent population evidence. [NIST exact binomial limits](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbici.htm).

The existing PRD's one-false-alert-per-20-benign-trials and 90%-known-case targets are prototype goals. They are not sufficient evidence of tolerable daily office behavior. Under a purely illustrative 100-benign-encounter day, a 5% alert probability would mean five unwanted alerts in expectation. This is arithmetic, not an estimate of actual office traffic or Airlock performance.

**Decision before further implementation.** Retain the evidence-driven incident workflow and the narrow managed-action gate as promising hypotheses. Resolve the accessible-owner-session and existing-app gaps first; establish a quiet policy for ordinary coworkers; validate actual intervention and recovery; and prove any theft-detection claim independently. Run a realistic baseline comparison and a workplace usability pilot before investing in more recognition, modes, hardware or model complexity. If the added context does not improve response enough to justify interruptions and setup, narrow the product to the part that does.
